Fold or Fail: Predicting Protein Stability Changes from Mutations

We test whether graph neural networks on residue contact graphs improve prediction of mutation-induced protein stability changes beyond strong condition-aware tabular baselines.

Why protein stability prediction is hard

A single amino acid substitution can change how a protein folds, but the effect depends on the mutation, the surrounding residue environment, and the broader structural context.

Flat mutation features as a baseline

We first tested tabular models using engineered mutation and condition features. These baselines define how much signal can be captured without explicit graph message passing.

Residue contact graphs and GNNs

We then represented proteins as residue contact graphs and trained GNN variants that differ in local readout, global pooling, and condition-feature integration.

What we found

The best full-test model was a condition-aware tabular baseline, while GNN variants remained competitive and revealed when structural graph information helped or failed to help.
